## Chapter 29. ELECTROMAGNETIC WAVES — THE TRANSMISSION OF VIBRATIONS

When it comes to electromagnetic waves, modern physics confidently uses formulas and terminology. But if we ask: what exactly is moving? — no clear mechanical answer is given.

We are told that an electromagnetic wave propagates.
We are told that an electric field generates a magnetic field, and a magnetic field generates an electric field.

But what are these fields?
What are they made of?
What exactly is oscillating?
What transmits the effect from point to point?
There is no answer in the mechanical sense.

Words are used, mathematics is used, but the carrier is not described, contact is not described, pressure is not described. That fundamental triad required to understand a process is missing.

In the aol model, everything becomes simpler.
An electromagnetic wave is not something special. It is the same process as light, but at different frequencies.
It is the transmission of vibrations through aol space.
Nothing is flying anywhere.
No particles are being emitted.
No “fields” are running through space on their own.

There are only vibrations of the medium.

Let us consider the source — an antenna.
When alternating current flows in an antenna, the atoms of the conductor begin to oscillate. These oscillations are transmitted to aol space — the atoms literally “strike” the aols, creating a sequence of compressions and expansions in the medium.

Each aol, receiving the impact, passes it on to neighboring aols.
This is how the wave arises.
It is not a transverse wave as typically depicted in textbooks. It is a longitudinal wave — a wave of compression and expansion, like sound, but propagating not in air, but in aol space.

Why is the speed of this wave equal to the speed of light?
Because the mechanism is the same.
Both light and electromagnetic waves are transmissions of vibrations from aol to aol. The speed is determined by the properties of the aols themselves — their stiffness, their ability to deform, and the rate at which they transmit impacts.

The source may differ, the frequencies may differ, but the transmission mechanism is the same.
That is why the speed is the same.
The difference between light and radio waves is not in their nature, but in frequency.
High-frequency vibrations are perceived by the eye as light.
Low-frequency vibrations are radio waves, invisible to the eye but detectable by antennas.
Their nature is the same — vibrations of the medium.

Thus, there is no need to introduce mysterious entities:

there is no need to explain how an electric field “creates” a magnetic field,

no need to explain how “empty space oscillates,”

no need to invent what moves in the absence of a carrier.

There is a medium.
There are vibrations.
There is transmission of motion.
And that is enough.

Electromagnetic waves are not a special phenomenon. They are a natural extension of the same mechanism we have already seen in light, heat, and electricity.
They are simply vibrations of aol space.

**Antenna — Geometry of Interaction with the Medium**

In modern physics, an antenna is usually described in terms of parameters, impedance, matching, and radiation patterns. All of this works, but if we ask: what does an antenna physically do? — there is again no mechanical answer.

In the aol model, the answer is simple.
An antenna is a device that strikes the aol space in a specific way.
When alternating current flows through an antenna, its atoms begin to oscillate. These oscillations are transmitted into aol space. But the transmission does not occur arbitrarily — it follows the geometry of the conductor.

And here, shape becomes the key factor.
If we take a straight conductor, its atoms oscillate along its length. This means that impacts on aol space occur primarily along that axis. As a result, a directed propagation of vibrations is formed — the antenna begins to “radiate” in specific directions.

If the antenna is bent, formed into a loop, or has a complex shape, the pattern of impacts on the medium changes. Vibrations are transmitted differently, and the wave acquires a different geometry.
Thus, an antenna does not “emit a wave” — it defines the geometry of vibration transmission in the medium.

This also explains another important aspect — antenna length.
Why are antennas made with lengths related to wavelength?
Because an antenna operates efficiently when its dimensions are matched to the frequency of oscillations.
If the antenna length corresponds to half a wavelength (or a multiple of it), the oscillations of its atoms combine coherently. Impacts on aol space reinforce each other rather than cancel out. The transmission becomes maximally efficient.

If the length is not matched, different parts of the antenna operate out of phase. Some regions reinforce the vibrations, others partially cancel them. Efficiency drops sharply.
Thus, an antenna is not just a piece of metal — it is a resonant system matched to the oscillations of the medium.

Even more simply:
An antenna is a tool that: defines the direction of impacts on the medium, defines their frequency, ensures their coherence.

That is why it can both transmit and receive.
When external vibrations of aol space reach the antenna, they begin to act on its atoms. If the shape and size of the antenna are matched to these vibrations, the atoms begin to oscillate in resonance. The aol “snake” begins to move — a current appears in the antenna.

The antenna converts vibrations of the medium into motion within the conductor.
And vice versa.
Motion in the conductor is converted into vibrations of the medium.
It is the same process — just in two directions.

**Radio Communication — Transmission of Vibrations Through a Medium**

Radio communication may seem almost magical: one device “sends a signal,” another, located hundreds or thousands of kilometers away, “receives” it.
In the classical description, it is said that an “electromagnetic wave” is radiated into space, carrying “energy and information.” 

But what exactly is being transmitted?
What moves from one antenna to another?
What carries the information?
Where is this carrier?
There is no answer in the mechanical sense.

There are formulas, calculations, and working technology — but no description of what physically happens.

In the aol model, this gap disappears.
Radio communication is not the transmission of “something through emptiness.”
It is the transmission of vibrations through a medium.

Let us begin with the transmitter.
An alternating current is created in the transmitter. But by itself, it does not yet carry information. Information appears when we begin to modify this current — its amplitude, frequency, or phase.
This means the antenna begins to oscillate not just rhythmically, but with a changing pattern of motion.
The atoms of the antenna strike the aol space, creating waves of compression and expansion. But now these oscillations carry structure — variations in which the information is encoded.

Next comes propagation.
Vibrations are transmitted from aol to aol. Nothing flies. No particles leave the antenna and travel through space.
Only motion is transmitted.
The medium simply reproduces, step by step, what was initiated at the source.
These vibrations reach the receiving antenna.

Now the reverse process occurs.
The aol space begins to act on the atoms of the antenna. If the antenna is tuned to the frequency of the incoming vibrations, its atoms begin to oscillate in resonance.

The aol “snake” begins to move — an electric current arises.
But this current is not random. It already contains the same pattern of variations that was encoded in the transmitter. The circuitry extracts these variations — and we hear sound, see images, or receive data.

Now we can state the key point.
Radio communication is not the transfer of a signal.
It is the reproduction of motion.
The transmitter defines motion in the medium.
The medium transmits this motion.
The receiver reproduces it.
There is no discontinuity in this process.

Now consider it from a common-sense perspective.
How can “emptiness” carry anything?
How can a “field” exist without a carrier?
How can transmission occur without contact?
To transmit motion, a carrier is required.
To exert influence, contact is required.
For something to happen, pressure is required.

And that is exactly what we see.
The transmitting antenna exerts pressure on the medium.
The medium transmits this pressure.
The receiving antenna converts it back into motion.
Everything fits into one simple scheme: contact — pressure — motion.
Nothing more.

Radio communication ceases to be mysterious once we stop asking “what is flying” and instead examine how motion is transmitted.
And then it becomes clear: there is no “jump through emptiness,” no “field magic” — only continuous transmission of vibrations through aol space.

**Spectrum of Vibrations — A Unified Scale**

In physics, when we speak of radio waves, light, ultraviolet radiation, or X-rays, they are usually presented as different phenomena grouped into the “electromagnetic spectrum.”

But if we think carefully, the same question arises: what exactly differs?
In the aol model, the answer is extremely simple.
What differs is the frequency of vibrations.
And nothing else.

In all these cases, the same process occurs — the transmission of vibrations through aol space. The only difference is how often the micro-impacts occur, how frequently the medium compresses and expands.

If the frequencies are low — these are radio waves.  
If higher — infrared vibrations, perceived as heat.  
Higher still — visible light.  
Higher — ultraviolet.  
Then X-rays and the gamma range.

But the nature of all these phenomena is the same.
There are no separate “types of waves.”
There are no different entities for radio, light, and X-rays.
There is only a single scale of vibrations in the medium.

It is like sound.
Low frequencies are bass, high frequencies are high-pitched tones — but the medium is the same (air), and the mechanism is the same: compression and expansion.
The same applies here.

Aol space is a single medium.
Vibrations are a single process.
Frequency is the only changing parameter.
This clarifies an important point.
Why does the eye see only a narrow range?
Because the eye is a receiver tuned to specific frequencies.
This is not a property of “light.”
It is a property of the eye.
If evolution had taken a different path, we might “see” radio waves as light or perceive X-rays as heat. But nature has given us only a narrow sensory range.

Everything else is the same vibrations — just outside our perception.
There is another important conclusion.
The higher the frequency of vibrations, the stronger their effect on matter.
Because impacts occur more frequently.
Low frequencies gently oscillate atoms, while high-frequency vibrations begin to strongly affect their structure. They can break bonds, alter atomic configurations and destroy matter.

In the aol model, this is explained simply: not as “more energetic quanta,” but as more frequent and intense воздействия of the medium.
Thus, the entire “electromagnetic spectrum” is not a set of different phenomena, but a unified picture.
One process. One medium. One nature.
Only the frequency changes.
And once this is understood, there is no need to divide the world into “radio,” “light,” and “radiation.”
There are only vibrations of aol space — from slow to extremely fast.
