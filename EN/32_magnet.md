## Chapter 32. MAGNET. INTERACTION OF MAGNETS

A magnet is one of the most familiar and at the same time one of the least explained objects in standard physics. Everyone has held it in their hands. Everyone has felt how it attracts iron and how two magnets repel each other.

But what exactly happens inside a magnet, and how does it act through space?
There is no clear mechanical explanation.
In the aol model, the mechanism is explained.

**What Happens Inside a Magnet**

Let us start with atoms. Atoms of different substances have different structures — and different abilities for the rotation of their parts. Iron atoms have a special feature: their parts are capable of rotating along the axis of the magnet’s body — longitudinally. This is a fundamental difference from atoms of copper or aluminum, where rotation is only transverse.

In an ordinary piece of iron, these atoms are oriented chaotically — in all directions. Their longitudinal rotations are directed differently and cancel each other out. There is no net effect.

But when iron becomes magnetized — under the influence of an external magnetic field or an electric current — the atoms gradually align in one direction. Their longitudinal rotations become coordinated.

And at that moment, something qualitatively new arises.
The coordinated rotating parts of the atoms begin to drive the aols of space in one direction — along the magnet. The aols move through the body of the magnet from one end to the other, exit outward, encounter the aol space, flow around the magnet externally, and return back. A closed, stable circulation of aols is formed.

This is a permanent magnet. 
Not an object with two poles, but an object within which a continuous closed flow of aols of space exists.

**What Are Poles**

The north pole is the place where aols exit the magnet outward.
The south pole is where they re-enter back inside.
These are not two separate parts of the magnet. They are the inlet and outlet of a single “pump”.

**Why Can You Not Cut Off a Magnetic Pole**

There is a simple experiment that confounds intuition. Take a magnet and cut it in half — expecting to obtain a separate north pole and a separate south pole. But each half immediately becomes a complete magnet with its own north and south poles. Cut it again — the same result. No matter how many times you divide it, it is impossible to obtain a “pure” pole.

The reason is that poles are misunderstood from the very beginning. They are perceived as parts of the magnet — as if north and south were two separate elements that could be detached. But this is not the case. Poles are not structural components. They are manifestations of a single closed process.

In the aol model, a magnet is not an object with two poles, but an object within which a stable, ordered motion of the aol space exists. The atoms of the magnet, due to their ability for longitudinal rotation, organize aols into directed flows — outward from the north pole, along the outside of the magnet toward the south pole, inward through the south pole, and back through the body of the magnet to the north pole. This is a closed circulation. It cannot have a beginning or an end — because the aol medium is continuous, and pressure within it does not allow discontinuities. Any break would be instantly compensated by a reconfiguration of the medium.

When a magnet is cut, the closed system of motion is disrupted. But the aol space immediately restructures — and in each fragment a new closed structure forms. Cutting does not destroy the mechanism of magnetization. It merely changes the geometry — and the system instantly adapts, creating two new closed loops instead of one.

Obtaining a monopole — a magnet with only one pole — is impossible not because “nature is built that way” in some abstract sense. It is because closed motion in a continuous medium cannot be broken. 

You cannot cut off the inlet of a pump from its outlet and leave only the inlet — the pump would simply cease to be a pump. The same applies to a magnet: cutting off one pole would mean breaking the closed process. But the medium does not allow this — it immediately closes the loop again.

**Magnetic Field Around a Magnet**

What is called the magnetic field is precisely this flow of aols outside the magnet. Aols exit from the north pole, spread into space, flow around the magnet, and enter the south pole. This is why iron filings scattered around a magnet arrange themselves into characteristic curved lines — they simply show the direction of aol motion in space.

This field is not created out of nothing. It does not appear instantly from nowhere. It is a redistribution of already existing aols of space — the same ones that fill everything around us. The magnet does not produce new matter. It organizes what already exists.

**Why Does a Magnet Attract Iron**

When a piece of iron enters the flow of aols created by a magnet, this flow begins to act on the iron’s atoms — causing their parts to rotate in one direction. The atoms of iron begin to align in a coordinated way, meaning they become temporarily magnetized. And now we have two objects with coordinated aol flows — the magnet and the magnetized iron.

Between them, a region forms where aols move in a coordinated way, and the pressure is lower than outside. The aol space outside presses more strongly — and presses the iron toward the magnet. It does not attract. It presses.

**Why Do Two Magnets Repel with Like Poles**

When two north poles approach each other, both magnets push aols toward one another. The two flows collide head-on. The pressure between the magnets sharply increases — higher than outside. The aol space between the magnets pushes them apart.

When a north and a south pole approach, the situation is reversed. The north pole of one magnet pushes aols out — the south pole of the other receives them. The flows do not collide but align — one continues the other. The pressure between the magnets drops below the external pressure. The aol space outside presses them together.

There is no attraction. There is a difference in pressure.

**Why Heating and Impact Demagnetize a Magnet**

A permanent magnet appears stable and almost eternal. It retains its properties for years. But if it is strongly heated or sharply struck, its magnetic properties weaken or disappear completely.

Why?
A magnet is not a thing with poles. It is a state of matter. Inside it, atoms are oriented in a coordinated way — their rotating parts all point in the same direction, collectively organizing the aols of space into a single ordered flow. As long as this coordination is preserved, the magnet exists. Once it is disrupted, the magnet disappears as a phenomenon, although the material itself remains.

Heating is an increase in vibrations. The aol space begins to shake the atoms more intensely. Up to a certain temperature, the system holds — internal bonds are strong enough to maintain coordination despite increased vibrations. But when the critical temperature — the Curie point — is reached, chaos overcomes order. Atoms begin to deviate from the coordinated direction, turn randomly, and the unified structure collapses. The magnetic state disappears.

Impact acts differently — not gradually, but instantly. A sharp mechanical force creates a wave of displacements within the material. Atoms that were aligned become abruptly shifted from their positions. Coordination is disrupted — the structure collapses. Even if the magnet appears intact externally, its internal structure is already disordered.

Both effects do the same thing through different mechanisms. Heating destroys order through accumulated vibrational chaos. Impact destroys it through sudden displacement. The result is the same — coordinated motion turns into chaotic motion, and the magnet ceases to be a magnet.

**How Magnetization Occurs**

If a magnet can be demagnetized, then it can also be created. How does an ordinary piece of iron become a magnet?

The answer begins with the fact that iron already contains everything necessary for magnetism. Its atoms are capable of longitudinal rotation — precisely the property that allows organizing the aols of space into directed flows. But in the normal state, these atoms are oriented chaotically. Their individual contributions cancel each other out, and there is no net magnetic field. Iron can be a magnet, but it is not one due to the lack of coordination.

To create a magnet, a directing influence must be introduced — bringing another magnet close, passing an electric current, or placing the iron in an external magnetic field. This influence creates an ordered motion of the aol space within the material — directed vortices and flows of aols that begin to act on the atoms of iron.

The atoms begin to rotate. Not all at once — the process is gradual. First, separate regions appear where atoms become aligned. These regions grow, capture neighboring atoms, and merge. When a sufficiently large part of the structure becomes coordinated, a single closed loop of aol motion forms, and the material becomes a magnet.

Once the structure is established, it becomes stable on its own. Atoms in the coordinated state support each other — each is held by the pressure of the aol space and the geometry of neighboring atoms. Therefore, even after removing the external influence, the magnet retains its state. This is a permanent magnet.

Magnetization is not the addition of something new. It is the reorganization of what already exists.

A chaotic structure becomes coordinated. And now the full picture becomes clear: magnetization and demagnetization are the same process in two directions — the creation of order and its destruction. 
A magnet exists as long as coordination exists. 
And it disappears when that coordination is destroyed.

**Main Conclusion**

A permanent magnet is not a mysterious object endowed with mystical attraction. It is a piece of matter in which atoms, in a coordinated way, organize a closed flow of aols of space. This flow creates a pressure difference around the magnet — and it is precisely this pressure difference that is responsible for everything we call magnetic phenomena.

No action at a distance.

No matter created from nothing. 

Only the motion of aols — contact and pressure.
