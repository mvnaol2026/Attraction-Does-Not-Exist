## Chapter 33. ETHER

### The Rise, Fall, and Hidden Return of the Aether: A Chronicle of the Universe's Primary Substance

For two and a half thousand years, the central debate in physics revolved around a single fundamental question: what fills the space between the stars and planets? The human mind, accustomed to tangible mechanics, refused to believe that one body could push or pull another across an absolute void. To transfer momentum, a material medium is required. In the history of science, this medium became the aether—an all-pervading substance that underwent several radical transformations over two millennia, changing its properties from solid crystal to viscous pitch, until scientists attempted to replace it with pure geometry and equations.

### The First Era: Solid Celestial Crystal (4th Century BC – 1577)

Aristotle first introduced the aether as a strict physical category in the 4th century BC. The ancient worldview divided the Universe into two non-overlapping tiers. Below, in the "sublunary world" of Earth, everything consisted of four coarse, mutable elements: earth, water, air, and fire. Yet above the Moon's orbit, in the "superlunary world," began the realm of eternity and ideal order, filled with a fifth element (quintessence)—the aether (translated from Greek as "pure upper air" or "always running").

The ancient aether possessed unique properties unattainable on Earth: it had neither heaviness nor lightness, did not age, did not heat up, and could only move in perfectly uniform circles. In the 2nd century AD, Claudius Ptolemy gave the aether a rigid mechanical form in his work Planetary Hypotheses. To explain why the planets do not fall to Earth, Ptolemy constructed the Universe as a giant nesting doll of solid, completely transparent aethereal spheres. The planets were embedded within the thickness of these crystal shells like precious stones in a ring. The spheres fit tightly against one another without a single gap, transferring rotation via mechanical friction from the outer sphere of the stars to the inner orbits.

This model endured for nearly two thousand years until 1577, when the Danish astronomer Tycho Brahe dealt it a fatal blow. Observing a bright comet and accurately measuring its distance, Brahe proved that the comet was traveling far beyond the Moon—passing directly through the orbits of Venus and Mars. The comet did not shatter against the celestial vaults, which meant that no solid crystal spheres existed in the sky.

### The Second Era: From Soft Fluid to Descartes' Vortices (1577 – 1687)

Following the collapse of the solid spheres, physics entered a transitional period in search of a new celestial mechanics. Tycho Brahe himself (1577–1601) suggested that the aether was not solid crystal, but an ultra-fine, soft, and completely permeable heavenly fluid through which planets and comets swim freely, like fish in water.

In 1609, his student Johannes Kepler—who discovered that planets move not in circles, but in elongated ellipses—attempted to find a new driving force within this medium. Kepler concluded that the rotating Sun emits invisible magnetic vortices (rays) around itself, which, like the spokes of a giant wheel, catch hold of the planets and drag them along their orbits.

A comprehensive mechanical picture of the aethereal world was created by the French philosopher and physicist René Descartes in 1644. Descartes flatly denied the very possibility of a void: by his logic, if there is nothing between two bodies, they must be touching. Therefore, Descartes packed the entire cosmos tightly with microscopic, mutually touching particles of aether. In this system, stars and planets moved because they floated inside gigantic aethereal whirlpools (vortices), like woodchips in a river current. The Cartesian worldview appeared remarkably visual and intuitive, allowing it to captivate all of continental Europe for an entire century.

### The Third Era: Newton's Void vs. "Repairing" the Vortices (1687 – 1750s)

In 1687, Isaac Newton published his magnum opus, Mathematical Principles of Natural Philosophy (Principia), which split the scientific world in two. Newton mathematically proved that Descartes' vortices could not work: inside a whirlpool, heavy and light planets would be swept along by the current differently, and retrograde comets would be blown away altogether. Newton took a revolutionary and deeply controversial step—for the first time in history, he completely banished the aether from celestial mechanics. In his model, planets flew by inertia through an absolute void, held in place by the mysterious force of gravity acting instantaneously at a distance without any material medium.

Continental Europe met Newton's void with fierce hostility, dismissing attraction across nothingness as a regression to medieval magic. For half a century, the greatest minds of the era tried to rescue and "repair" aethereal mechanics:

**•	In 1689, Gottfried Leibniz** divided the aether into two independent layers (one for light, another for gravity), attempting to mathematically match the speeds of the vortices to planetary orbits.

**•	In 1690, Christiaan Huygens** replaced Descartes' slow whirlpools with a chaotic swarm of ultra-tiny aether particles darting about at tremendous speeds. Driven by centrifugal force, these particles tended to fly upward away from the Earth, pushing ordinary heavy bodies downward—thus Huygens mechanically explained falling objects without any need for attraction.

**•	In the 1730s, Johann Bernoulli** proposed a "spongy" aether—an ultra-fine medium capable of flowing freely through the pores inside atoms like water through a fishing net, transmitting gravitational pressure without slowing down the planets.

By the 1740s, however, the vortex aether had lost the battle. Saving it required far too many artificial caveats, whereas Newton's formulas of the void predicted the motion of planets and comets with impeccable precision. For the sake of mathematical accuracy, science sacrificed an intuitive mechanism, and the gravitational aether was sent into retirement.

### The Fourth Era: The Paradoxical Luminiferous Aether (1801 – 1887)

Having banished the aether from planetary mechanics, physicists in the early 19th century discovered they could not do without it in optics. Between 1801 and 1818, Thomas Young and Augustin-Jean Fresnel irrefutably proved that light is not a stream of particles (as Newton had believed), but a wave. And every wave requires a material medium to oscillate. Thus, the luminiferous aether took the stage, and attempts to describe its physical properties led science into a profound paradox.

Experiments with the polarization of light showed that a light wave is transverse—it oscillates perpendicular to its direction of travel, much like a wave along a taut string. Transverse waves do not occur in gases or liquids; they exist only in solid elastic bodies. Given the colossal speed of light (300,000 kilometers per second), this solid aether had to possess an elasticity billions of times greater than that of tempered steel. Yet at the same time, the Earth was hurtling through this "ultra-solid crystal" at a speed of 30 kilometers per second without the slightest friction or drag.

To reconcile the properties of a diamond with those of an absolute void, British physicist George Stokes proposed the "pitch-aether" model in 1845 (by analogy with shoemaker's wax). If you strike a lump of pitch with a hammer—a rapid impact akin to a high-frequency light wave—it shatters like solid glass. Yet if you place a lead ball on top of it and wait for months—a slow motion akin to a planet's flight—the ball will sink smoothly into it without any resistance. Furthermore, Stokes believed that as the Earth moves, it drags an adjacent layer of this viscous aether along with it, like a sphere coated in honey.

### The Fifth Era: The Great Turning Point and the Abolition of the Medium (1887 – 1905)

By the end of the 19th century, most physicists—including James Clerk Maxwell, who had formulated the equations of electromagnetism specifically as the mechanics of tensions within the aether—believed that the luminiferous aether stood completely stationary throughout the Universe while material bodies moved through it. Then, in 1887, American physicists Albert Michelson and Edward Morley conducted a decisive experiment. They set out to measure the head-on "aether wind" generated by the Earth's orbit around the Sun by comparing the speed of light traveling along the Earth's motion with that traveling perpendicular to it. The result stunned the scientific world: the speed of light proved to be completely identical in every direction. The apparatus detected no aether wind whatsoever.

To rescue the concept of a stationary aether, Dutch physicist Hendrik Lorentz and Irishman George FitzGerald (between 1892 and 1904) offered a purely mechanical explanation for the experiment's failure. Lorentz argued that the head-on pressure of the aether physically compresses (flattens) the electron shells of atoms within a moving body along its direction of travel and slows down their internal oscillations. The apparatus's measuring rod physically shortened by just enough to mask the difference in the speed of the light beams. It was specifically to describe this physical contraction within a medium that Lorentz derived his famous mathematical formulas—the Lorentz transformations.

In 1905, however, 26-year-old Albert Einstein staged a radical coup. He took the ready-made formulas of Lorentz and Henri Poincaré, yet completely discarded the aether itself as a "superfluous" entity. Einstein declared that bodies contract and time slows down not due to the resistance of a real material medium, but simply because such is the geometry of empty space and time, in which the speed of light must, by postulate, remain constant for any observer. Once again, science favored a convenient mathematical abstraction over the search for a tangible physical carrier.

### The Sixth Era: The Hidden Return of the Aether Under New Names (1920 – Present Day)

After 1905, the word "aether" fell under an unspoken ban in mainstream science, becoming synonymous with outdated views. Yet the problem of the void itself never went away—it simply changed its nameplate.

Curiously, as early as 1920, in his famous Leiden address, Albert Einstein himself walked back his earlier stance. While developing the general theory of relativity, which explained gravity as the "curvature" of spacetime, he was forced to admit that it is impossible to curve absolute nothingness. Einstein publicly stated: "According to the general theory of relativity, space without aether is unthinkable," proposing to understand it as the metric field itself—endowed with physical properties, yet devoid of a conventional mechanical structure.

In the second half of the 20th century and into the 21st century, the "abolished" aether made a definitive comeback in theoretical physics, though now under the names of the quantum vacuum, fundamental fields (the 17 fields of the Standard Model), the Higgs field (an invisible, all-pervading "jelly" in which particles bog down, thereby acquiring mass), and dark energy pushing the Universe apart.

Today, mainstream science maintains that no void exists—every point in space is filled with continuous fields, and the particles of matter familiar to us are merely local "ripples" and excitations of these fields. Having abandoned a material, granular aether at the beginning of the 20th century, physics ultimately arrived at a "mathematical aether"—a system of invisible media lacking a material carrier, leaving the central question regarding the mechanical nature of the world's primary substance wide open.

### The Main Oddities and Logical Dead Ends of the Official Rejection of the Aether

**•	The Paradox of "Immaterial Elasticity":**    
Modern cosmology claims that empty spacetime is capable of expanding, contracting, and even vibrating. Yet only a real material medium possessing a modulus of elasticity and density can expand, contract, and vibrate. Claiming that "empty nothingness" vibrates is a logical absurdity. Science has attributed every property of the aether to the void, while forbidding anyone from calling it matter.

**•	The Dead End of 25 Invisible "Bed Sheets":**    
Having rejected a single unified substance, quantum field theory was forced to assert that roughly two and a half dozen independent fundamental fields (corresponding to the number of elementary particle types) are superimposed on one another at every single point in space. Instead of one understandable material medium, physicists had to introduce dozens of invisible entities occupying the exact same volume.

**•	Ignoring Local Causality:**    
Without a continuous medium, mainstream science has slid into the mysticism of contactless action at a distance: charges attract one another across a vacuum, and quantum particles instantaneously "teleport." By removing the material intermediary, physics removed the very mechanics of contiguous action.

Modern science has driven itself into a methodological trap. It relies on the properties of a universal medium in every field equation and cosmological model, yet categorically refuses to acknowledge its material nature or search for its real physical Prime Mover—for such an admission would demand a complete overhaul of the foundations of relativity and quantum field theory.
