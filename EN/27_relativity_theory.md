## Chapter 27. HOW RELATIVITY THEORY EMERGED — AND WHAT WOULD HAPPEN IF A MEDIUM EXISTED

At the end of the 19th century, physics faced a serious contradiction. On one side was the principle of relativity formulated by Galileo Galilei, known for two centuries: the laws of nature are the same for all observers moving uniformly and in straight lines. In a train moving at constant speed, a cup of tea behaves exactly as it does on the platform. No experiment inside the carriage can determine whether the train is moving or standing still.

On the other side were the equations of James Clerk Maxwell describing electromagnetism. They implied that the speed of light is about 300,000 km/s. But relative to what? If relative to the observer, then an observer moving toward the light beam should measure a higher speed, and one moving in the same direction should measure a lower one. This seemed obvious — this is how all velocities behave in classical mechanics.

However, the Michelson–Morley experiment showed something entirely different. No matter how precisely measurements were made, the speed of light remained the same in all directions. The Earth moves around the Sun at 30 km/s — yet the speed of light does not change at all.

Imagine the situation physicists faced at the time. If you are driving a car and turn on the headlights, then according to Isaac Newton, the light from your headlights should travel faster than light from a roadside lamp — because the car’s speed adds to the speed of light. But experiments showed: no. The speed of light is the same for everyone — whether standing still or moving toward the beam.

Albert Einstein solved this puzzle in a radical way. He accepted the constancy of the speed of light as an axiom — a fundamental law of nature requiring no explanation. Then he asked: if the speed of light is constant, what must change?

The answer was unexpected — time and space must change.
Time can slow down.
Objects can contract.
Simultaneity depends on the observer.

Thus, in 1905, the special theory of relativity was born.
Mathematically, it is flawless.
Technologically, it works — without relativistic corrections, GPS navigation would accumulate errors of several kilometers within a day. But mechanically, it left a vast gap.

Why does time slow down?
What exactly happens to a moving clock?
What physically contracts a moving rocket?
These questions are answered mathematically — but not mechanically.

Einstein did the only thing he could after the ether was abandoned. By removing the medium, he lost the mechanism and had to replace it with geometry. Space-time bends, stretches, and contracts.
This works mathematically, but is physically difficult to imagine.

**Why a Maximum Speed Exists**

Can a body accelerate indefinitely? At first glance — yes. If a force is continuously applied, velocity should increase without limit. This is how Newtonian mechanics works in empty space without resistance.

Each aol requires time to receive the impact, deform, and pass the motion to the next one. A real aol does so with a delay. This delay determines the speed of propagation of any interaction in the medium.

As speed increases, the situation changes. The body begins to move faster than the medium can reorganize behind it. Wedges form less effectively — aols do not have time to fill the space behind the body before it moves forward. The support weakens. Acceleration decreases.

At higher speeds, a limit is reached. The aol medium can no longer form new wedges at the required rate — the reconfiguration speed of the medium is exhausted. The support from behind stops increasing. Acceleration ceases.

This limit is the speed of light.
