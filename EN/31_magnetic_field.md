## Chapter 31. MAGNETIC FIELD

We already know that current is the motion of aols, that this motion is created by a pressure difference, and that the flow can be directed. Now a new phenomenon appears.

If current flows through a conductor, what is called a “magnetic field” arises around it. It acts on other conductors, deflects needles, and produces forces. But what is it, physically?

If we remove the word “field,” it becomes clear that it explains nothing. It is merely a name for an observed effect. What we need is a mechanism.

In the aol model, the magnetic field is a vortex motion of aol space. It is not a separate entity and not an “invisible force,” but a real motion of the medium.
When current flows through a conductor, aols move along it and interact with atoms. 
Due to the structure of matter, not only linear but also rotational motion arises. The flow twists, forming a vortex around the conductor.

This is a natural result: the motion is not perfectly linear, there is interaction with the surroundings, and structural elements allow for rotation. As a result, the flow of aols becomes twisted.

A specific direction of rotation is observed. In classical physics, this is called the “right-hand rule,” but mechanically it is simply the direction of the vortex.

This vortex does not remain local. 
It propagates outward, involving neighboring aols and spreading into the surrounding space.
As the distance from the conductor increases, the vortex motion weakens. 
The rotation diminishes, transmission decreases, and the vortex dissipates.

If another conductor is nearby, its structure begins to interact with this vortex. Pressure and motion arise. 
In other words, the vortex of one flow affects another.

Magnets behave in the same way. 
Their atomic structure is organized such that rotational motion is stable. Therefore, persistent vortices arise around them.
Magnetism ceases to be a mystery. There is no need to introduce “fields without carriers” or “magnetic forces.” 

There is motion of the medium.
Now it becomes clear that electricity is linear motion of aols, while magnetism is vortex motion of aols. 

These are two modes of the same process.
The main conclusion: the magnetic field is a vortex motion of aol space arising from the motion of aols in matter.
