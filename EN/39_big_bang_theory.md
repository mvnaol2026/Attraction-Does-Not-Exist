## Chapter 39. TWO FACTORS BEHIND THE EMERGENCE OF THE BIG BANG THEORY

The Big Bang theory in modern science is based on two main observed phenomena:

1. Cosmic microwave background radiation.  
2. Cosmological redshift.  
 
It is believed that these observations confirm the idea that the Universe originated from the explosion of some primordial “point” and has been expanding ever since.
Let us examine both factors and see what is actually observed and how it is interpreted.

**1. Cosmic microwave background radiation**

Modern instruments do indeed detect a weak, uniform influence coming from all directions in space. This influence is commonly called cosmic microwave background radiation and is interpreted as the “residual heat” from an ancient Big Bang.

According to the scientific picture, the early Universe was a hot plasma in which processes of emission, absorption, and re-emission of photons occurred. Over time, this plasma cooled, and at a certain temperature, electrons began to combine with protons, forming atoms. Photons were then “released” and have supposedly been traveling through the Universe ever since, gradually cooling due to its expansion.

However, upon closer examination, key questions arise.
First of all, the mechanism of these processes remains unclear. What does it mean for an electron to “absorb” a photon? In what form does the photon exist inside the electron? How does its subsequent “re-emission” occur? No mechanical explanation is provided — only descriptive formulations are used.

Another issue concerns temperature. It is said that particles cooled over hundreds of thousands of years. But what exactly is “cooling” in point-like particles? What is the carrier of heat? If heat is considered a manifestation of motion, then it must be specified what exactly is moving and due to what.

There is also another important point. It is claimed that photons emitted in the early Universe are still reaching the Earth today. But the Earth formed much later than those events. If photons truly travel at the speed of light, they should have long since left the region where the Earth later formed. This raises the question: what exactly are instruments detecting today?

Thus, the observed phenomenon exists, but its interpretation raises serious doubts.

Within the framework of the aol space hypothesis, this phenomenon is explained differently.
The Universe is a dense medium composed of aols, which are in constant contact and under pressure. This medium continuously vibrates. Oscillations arise everywhere — from the motion of stars, planets, atomic structures, and other processes.

As a result, a general background of oscillations is formed, present throughout all space. It is this background that instruments detect.

It is not a “relic” of some ancient event. It is the current, continuously existing state of the medium.

The inhomogeneity of this radiation also receives a natural explanation: in different regions of the Universe, the density of sources of oscillations varies, so the background slightly differs as well.

A temperature of about 2.7 K, in this case, is not “residual heat,” but a characteristic of the intensity of the background oscillations of the medium, which instruments interpret as thermal radiation.

Thus, the observed phenomenon is explained without invoking the hypothesis of a point explosion and without the need to introduce undescribed mechanisms of particle interaction.

**2. Cosmological redshift**

The second factor on which the Big Bang theory is based is the observed redshift in the spectra of distant galaxies.

This shift is interpreted as a consequence of the Doppler effect — galaxies are moving away from us, and the farther they are, the faster they recede. From this, the conclusion is drawn that the Universe is expanding.

Moreover, it is observed that this recession is accelerating. That is, galaxies are not just moving apart — they are speeding up.

To explain this fact, science introduces an additional concept — dark energy, some unknown substance uniformly distributed throughout the Universe and causing its accelerated expansion.

However, a number of questions arise here as well.
If we consider the expansion as the result of some initial explosion, it would be natural to expect that the maximum velocities were reached at the moment of that explosion, and that afterward the motion should slow down or, at least, remain uniform.

The observed acceleration, however, requires a constant force. But the nature of this force is not explained — only the term “dark energy” is introduced, without specifying its mechanism.

In the aol space hypothesis, the accelerated motion of cosmic objects is explained without introducing additional entities.

The Universe is considered as a medium with a non-uniform distribution of pressure. Within each “small universe,” the pressure of aol space is higher in central regions and lower at the periphery.

Any body in such a medium will be pushed out of the region of high pressure into a region of lower pressure. This is natural behavior in any medium.

Two factors act on cosmic objects.
The first is the pressure of the medium itself, pushing the object away from the center.

The second is the effect of aols flowing around the moving body. As the body moves, so-called aol “wedges” form behind it, which additionally push it forward.

It is important that the contribution of these “wedges” increases over time. The faster the object moves, the more frequently such effects are formed per unit time. This leads to an increase in the resulting pressure and, consequently, to an acceleration of motion.

Thus, acceleration has a specific mechanical cause related to the properties of the medium.
There is no need to introduce “dark energy” or to assume that space itself is expanding.

**Conclusion**

Both key arguments in favor of the Big Bang theory — cosmic microwave background radiation and redshift — are indeed based on observable phenomena.

However, their traditional interpretation relies on assumptions whose mechanisms are not described.

Within the framework of the aol space hypothesis, these same phenomena receive a different explanation based on:

• the existence of a medium,  
• its pressure,  
• and the transmission of motion through contact interactions.

At the same time, there is no need to introduce additional entities or processes that lack mechanical explanation.
This makes the explanation simpler, more consistent, and based on a single principle — the motion of matter through contact pressure.
